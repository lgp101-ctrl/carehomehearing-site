Questions to ask a care home about hearing care

Twelve questions to ask when you visit a care home, why each one matters, and what a good answer sounds like.

If your relative has hearing loss, or wears hearing aids, good hearing care makes a big difference to their daily life. These questions help you find out how a care home looks after residents’ hearing. Take them with you on a visit, or ask on the phone.

You do not need to ask them all. Start with the first five, which matter most day to day.

  1. Who checks and cleans residents' hearing aids each morning?Why it matters: Hearing aids only help if they are clean, working and put in correctly every day.A good answer: A named person or role does it as part of the morning routine, and it is written down.
  2. How do you find out about a new resident's hearing?Why it matters: Hearing needs should be known from the first day, not discovered weeks later.A good answer: Hearing is part of the assessment when someone moves in, and it is recorded in their care plan.
  3. What happens if a hearing aid stops working or goes missing?Why it matters: A broken or lost hearing aid can leave someone cut off from conversation for weeks.A good answer: Staff check simple things such as batteries and tubing first, then contact the NHS hearing service or private provider quickly.
  4. Do you keep spare hearing aid batteries, and who orders more?Why it matters: A flat battery is the most common reason a hearing aid stops working.A good answer: Spares are kept in a known place, and someone is responsible for ordering free NHS batteries in good time.
  5. How do you help residents get hearing tests and repairs?Why it matters: Hearing changes over time, and people living with dementia may not ask for help.A good answer: The home arranges NHS hearing tests and repairs, helps with travel or home visits, and follows up afterwards.
  6. How are your staff trained in hearing care?Why it matters: Many care staff have never had hearing care training.A good answer: Staff learn how to look after hearing aids and how to communicate well, and new staff learn it when they start.
  7. Does someone in the home lead on hearing care?Why it matters: A named lead, sometimes called a Hearing Loss Champion, keeps good habits going.A good answer: Yes. They check that daily routines happen and help other staff.
  8. How do staff talk with residents who find it hard to hear?Why it matters: Good communication helps, even when hearing aids are working.A good answer: Staff face the person, get their attention first, speak clearly without shouting, turn down background noise, and write things down if needed.
  9. Is there a quiet place for visits and conversations?Why it matters: Background noise from TVs, kitchens and corridors makes it much harder to hear.A good answer: There is a quiet room or corner away from the TV and other noise.
  10. How do you look after ear health and earwax?Why it matters: Earwax is common and can block hearing and hearing aids.A good answer: Staff notice the signs and arrange a check with a GP, practice nurse or hearing professional. Staff do not try to remove earwax themselves.
  11. How will you keep us informed about my relative's hearing?Why it matters: Families often notice changes first and can help.A good answer: You will be told about problems, appointments and changes, and asked what you have noticed.
  12. Do you have TV subtitles, a hearing loop or other listening help?Why it matters: Simple equipment can make TV, group activities and visits easier to follow.A good answer: Subtitles are used, and a hearing loop or personal listener is available where it helps.
