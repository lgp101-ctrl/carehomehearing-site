For families

How to communicate during visits

Simple changes can turn a tiring visit into a relaxed and enjoyable one, for both of you.

Before you start

  • Ask staff to check the hearing aids are in, switched on and working before you arrive, or check them together when you get there.
  • Choose a quiet place. A corner of a quiet lounge, their room or the garden is usually better than a busy lounge with the TV on.
  • Sit in good light with the light on your face, not behind you. Do not sit with your back to a window.

While you talk

  • Get their attention before you start: say their name, or gently touch their arm.
  • Face them at the same level, about an arm’s length away.
  • Speak clearly at a natural pace. Do not shout; it distorts your voice and face.
  • Rephrase rather than repeat if something is not understood.
  • One person at a time. If several of you visit, take turns to speak and avoid side conversations.
  • Give time for them to answer.
  • Use other cues: photos, a notepad, gestures and objects can all help.

Video calls and phone calls

  • Video calls are often easier than phone calls because your relative can see your face.
  • Turn on captions if the app has them.
  • Ask staff whether the hearing aids can connect to a tablet or phone; many modern hearing aids can.
  • Keep calls short if they are tiring.

Bring something to talk about

Photos, letters, a familiar object or music can make conversation easier and more enjoyable, especially for people living with dementia.

It's not your fault, or theirs

If a visit is difficult, try again another day with a different time or place. Many people with hearing loss tire quickly, so shorter, more frequent visits may work better.
