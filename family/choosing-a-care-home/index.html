Choosing a care home for someone with hearing loss or dementia

How to find a care home where your relative will be heard, what to look for on a visit, and how to share their hearing needs.

Choosing a care home is a big decision, often made quickly. If your relative has hearing loss, or lives with dementia, how a home looks after hearing can make the difference between joining in and being left out.

1. Start with what your relative needs

Write down a few things before you search:

  • Do they wear hearing aids? Who supplied them: the NHS or a private provider?
  • Do they have a cochlear implant, or use sign language?
  • Do they live with dementia? Hearing loss can make dementia symptoms seem worse, and the two need managing together.
  • What helps them most in conversation: quiet rooms, good light, people facing them, writing things down?

2. Search and make a shortlist

Search every care home in England by town, postcode or name. Each home shows its CQC rating and its hearing care:

  • Not yet shared means the home has not shared its hearing care with us yet. That does not mean its hearing care is poor: ask on your visit.
  • Shared by the home means the home has told us about its hearing care. It has not been independently checked.
  • Independently verified means an independent assessor visited and checked it against a published standard.

Add homes to your shortlist, then send it to the rest of your family.

3. Read the CQC report

Every home’s page links to its latest Care Quality Commission report. Look for what it says about communication, care plans and how staff treat residents.

4. Visit, and listen as well as look

Take the questions to ask with you. While you are there, notice:

  • Is the TV or music loud when nobody is watching or listening?
  • Do staff face residents, get their attention and speak clearly?
  • Is there a quiet room for visits?
  • Are hearing aids in, and do residents seem to follow conversations?

5. When your relative moves in

  • Tell the home about their hearing and hearing aids on the first day.
  • Fill in a hearing passport together and give it to the staff.
  • Ask who leads on hearing care, and how you will hear about any problems.
  • Read about dementia and hearing loss and communicating during visits.
