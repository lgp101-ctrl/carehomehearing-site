What to do when hearing aids are regularly lost

Lost hearing aids are upsetting and costly. Working with the care home to find the pattern usually solves it.

Hearing aids are small and easily lost, especially for people living with dementia. If it keeps happening, it is worth sitting down with the care home to work out why.

Common reasons

  • Hearing aids are taken out by your relative and put somewhere unusual
  • They are wrapped in tissues or left on meal trays and thrown away
  • They end up in bedding or clothing and go to the laundry
  • They are not put away in a consistent place at night
  • They go missing during hospital stays

Questions to ask the home

  • Is there a consistent routine for putting them in, taking them out and storing them?
  • Are bedding, trays and clothing checked before they go to the laundry or kitchen?
  • Is each loss recorded as an incident, so patterns can be spotted?
  • Is the reason my relative takes them out understood, for example discomfort, noise or not recognising them?

Practical solutions

  • Retention cords or clips that attach hearing aids to clothing. Ask the supplier.
  • A brightly coloured, labelled case that is easy to spot.
  • Hearing aid insurance or loss cover for privately bought aids.
  • A hearing aid passport that goes with your relative to hospital.
  • Reviewing the hearing aids themselves: a better fit or simpler device may mean they are removed less often.

Replacements

  • NHS hearing aids: contact the NHS audiology service. Some services charge for lost hearing aids, while others replace them free, at least the first time.
  • Private hearing aids: contact the provider. They may be covered by a warranty or insurance.

Work together

Repeated losses are rarely one person’s fault. A short meeting with the home’s Hearing Loss Champion or manager to agree a plan usually helps more than any single fix.
