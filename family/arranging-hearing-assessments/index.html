For families

How to arrange hearing assessments

The routes to a hearing test for someone living in a care home, and how to make the appointment go well.

When to ask for one

  • Your relative has never had a hearing test, or it has been a long time
  • Their hearing seems to have gradually got worse
  • Their hearing aids no longer seem to help
  • They have, or may have, dementia (NICE suggests considering a hearing assessment for people with dementia or memory problems)

Sudden hearing loss is different

If hearing changes suddenly, do not wait for a routine appointment. Ask the care home to contact the GP or NHS 111 the same day.

The routes

Through the NHS

  • The GP can refer your relative to the local NHS audiology service. The care home can ask the GP on your relative’s behalf.
  • In some areas, adults can be referred directly to NHS hearing services without seeing the GP first.
  • Some NHS services offer visits for people who cannot travel. Ask whether this is available locally.

Through a private provider

  • Your relative, or someone acting for them, can contact a private hearing care provider directly. Many offer visits to care homes.
  • Ask about costs, what is included and aftercare before agreeing.

Making the appointment go well

  • Earwax: ask whether the ears need checking for wax before the appointment.
  • Information: send the hearing passport, a list of medicines and any concerns.
  • Support: someone who knows your relative well should go with them or be present, especially if they have dementia.
  • Consent and capacity: your relative should be involved in decisions. If they may lack capacity, the professionals and care home should follow the Mental Capacity Act. If you hold a Lasting Power of Attorney for health and welfare, tell them.
  • Afterwards: make sure the results and any new hearing aid instructions are shared with the care home and added to the care plan.
