Understanding NHS and private hearing care

A balanced guide to the two routes, so your family can make the choice that is right for your relative.

In the UK, adults can get hearing tests and hearing aids through the NHS or from a private provider. Both have qualified, registered professionals. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on your relative’s needs, preferences and circumstances.

NHS hearing care

  • Free hearing assessments, hearing aids, batteries, repairs and aftercare.
  • Usually accessed through a GP referral. In some areas adults can be referred directly to NHS hearing services.
  • Provided by NHS audiology departments or by providers the NHS has contracted.
  • Modern digital hearing aids, usually behind-the-ear or receiver-in-canal styles, fitted to the person’s hearing test.
  • Some NHS services can visit people who cannot travel, including in care homes. Availability varies by area.
  • Waiting times vary by area.
  • Some services may charge for lost hearing aids.

Private hearing care

  • You pay for hearing aids, often with aftercare included for a set period.
  • A wider choice of styles and features, such as very small or in-the-ear aids, and some extra connectivity options.
  • Appointments may be available sooner, and many providers offer home and care home visits.
  • Prices and what is included vary a lot. Ask what aftercare, batteries, repairs and loss cover are included, and for how long.

Questions to help decide

  • What matters most: cost, speed, style, or home visits?
  • Can your relative manage the device, or will staff need to? Simpler can be better.
  • What support will be available if something goes wrong?
  • Are there any medical issues that need NHS or GP involvement first?

Your relative can use both

Some people have NHS hearing aids and use private services for other things, such as earwax removal, or the other way round. Whatever the route, tell the care home who supplied the hearing aids so staff know who to contact.

Professionals who test hearing and fit hearing aids in the UK should be registered, for example with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). You can ask any provider about their registration.
