Types of hearing aid, NHS and private

Learn to recognise the main types of hearing aid you will see in care homes, and the difference between NHS and privately supplied aids.

The main types

Behind-the-ear (BTE) with earmould. The hearing aid sits behind the ear. A clear tube carries sound to a custom-made earmould in the ear. Many NHS hearing aids are this type. Some BTE aids use a thin tube and dome instead of an earmould.

Receiver-in-canal (RIC). A small case sits behind the ear. A thin wire runs to the receiver (speaker), which sits in the ear canal covered by a soft dome or custom tip. RIC aids usually have a wax filter in the receiver.

Custom in-the-ear (ITE). The whole hearing aid is a shell made from a mould of the person’s ear. Smaller versions sit further into the canal. They often have a wax guard in the sound outlet and sometimes a small removal thread.

Rechargeable or battery

  • Rechargeable aids sit in a charger overnight. There is no battery door.
  • Battery aids have a small door, usually at the bottom, for a disposable zinc-air battery. Batteries need changing every few days to a couple of weeks.

You will learn about both in lesson 5.

NHS and private hearing aids

NHS hearing aids Private hearing aids
Cost Free on the NHS Bought from a private provider, often with aftercare included for a set time
Who fits them NHS audiology service, or a provider the NHS has contracted Private hearing care professional
Batteries, repairs, retubing Free from the NHS service that supplied them From the provider, sometimes included in the price
Quality Modern digital hearing aids Wide range of styles and features

Both NHS and private services use qualified, registered professionals. Neither is automatically better. What matters is that the hearing aids suit the person, are used and are well looked after.

Follow the instructions for each device

Every make and model is slightly different. Always follow the manufacturer’s instructions and the resident’s care plan. If you do not have the user guide, ask the supplier for one.

Check your understanding

Your answers are not recorded anywhere except, if possible, on this device.

Which type of hearing aid has a thin wire running into the ear canal with a dome on the end?

Receiver-in-canal (RIC) aids have the speaker (receiver) on the end of a thin wire in the ear canal, usually covered by a soft dome or custom tip.

Which statement about NHS and private hearing aids is fair?

NHS hearing aids are modern digital devices fitted by qualified audiologists. Private providers also use qualified, registered professionals. Neither is automatically better.
