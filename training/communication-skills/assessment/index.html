Module 6

Final assessment: Communication Skills in Care Homes

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. What should you do before starting an important conversation with a resident who wears hearing aids?

    A hearing aid that is out, switched off or not working makes every other technique less effective. Check it first.

  2. How should you get a resident's attention?

    Make sure the resident knows you are about to speak and can see your face before you begin.

  3. Where should you position yourself?

    Being at eye level with your face well lit makes lip-reading and facial expressions easier to see.

  4. A resident has not understood you after you repeated a sentence once. What should you do?

    Some words are harder to hear or lip-read. Rephrasing, gestures and writing help when repeating does not.

  5. Which of these makes lip-reading harder?

    Over-exaggerating distorts the natural shapes of speech, and covering your mouth hides them.

  6. The dining room is busy and noisy. Mr Evans, who has hearing loss, is struggling to follow conversation at his table.

    What would help most?

    Reducing background noise and seating people where they can see faces makes mealtimes more sociable and less tiring.

  7. What is the best way to check a resident has understood a change to their medication?

    Open questions and written notes are more reliable than yes/no questions, and they protect the resident's right to understand their own care.

  8. Mrs Taylor, who lives with dementia and hearing loss, becomes agitated in the lounge every afternoon.

    What should you check first?

    Distress can be a sign of unmet communication needs, such as hearing aid problems or too much noise. Check these first.

  9. How should a resident who cannot hear the fire alarm be alerted in an emergency?

    Every resident's evacuation plan should show how they will be alerted if they cannot hear alarms.

  10. Which statement about end-of-life care is correct?

    Many people can still hear near the end of life. Gentle talking, familiar voices and working hearing aids, if comfortable, can bring comfort.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
