Module 5

Final assessment: Hearing Aid Troubleshooting

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. What is the first step of the four-step hearing aid check?

    Start with power. A flat battery or uncharged aid is the most common cause of a "dead" hearing aid.

  2. A battery hearing aid has no sound. What should you do first?

    Fitting a new battery of the correct size solves most "no sound" problems in battery aids.

  3. Mr Collins's rechargeable aids were dead this morning, and the charger lights did not come on last night.

    What is the most likely cause?

    If the lights do not come on, check the plug, wall switch and that each aid is pushed fully into the charger.

  4. Which of these is safe for care staff to do?

    Changing filters and domes with the parts and tools supplied is safe. Opening, reprogramming or washing the aid is not.

  5. A hearing aid whistles when the resident is sitting in a high-backed chair. What is the likely cause?

    Anything pressing on the aid, such as a headrest, hat or pillow, can cause feedback. Repositioning usually solves it.

  6. How should a hearing aid that got wet be dried?

    Gentle drying with the battery door open is safe. Heat can damage the electronics and casing.

  7. A hearing aid is lost. What should happen first?

    Searching straight away, before the laundry and rubbish are collected, finds most lost hearing aids.

  8. Mrs Price refuses her hearing aids, saying everything is "too loud" in the dining room.

    What is the most helpful response?

    Noisy rooms are hard with hearing aids. The supplier can often adjust settings, and reducing background noise helps everyone.

  9. A privately bought hearing aid still does not work after the four-step check. Who should you contact?

    Private providers support the hearing aids they sold. NHS services support NHS hearing aids.

  10. During the "Person" check you find discharge on the earmould and the resident says the ear hurts. What should you do?

    Pain and discharge are health problems that need same-day medical advice. Keep the aid out of that ear until advised.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
