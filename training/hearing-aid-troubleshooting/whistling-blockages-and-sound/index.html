Whistling, blockages, moisture and poor sound

Whistling and muffled or crackly sound usually have simple causes. Learn to tell them apart and fix what is safe to fix.

Whistling (feedback)

Whistling happens when sound escapes from the ear and is picked up again by the hearing aid’s microphone.

Common causes and safe checks:

  • Not fully inserted: take the aid out and put it back in carefully.
  • Wrong ear: check red = right, blue = left.
  • Something pressing on it: a hat, high-backed chair or pillow. A short whistle during a hug is normal.
  • Blocked earmould, dome or filter: wax can bounce sound back out.
  • Split or loose tube: report it for replacement.
  • Earmould no longer fits: ears change shape over time, and earmoulds can shrink. The supplier can remake it.

Blocked tubing, earmoulds and wax filters

  • Wipe the earmould and clear its opening with the brush or tool supplied.
  • Change blocked wax filters and dirty domes using the parts made for that aid.
  • Report tubing that is hard, yellow, split or squashed.

Moisture

Sweat, steam and rain can get into hearing aids.

  • Take the aid out, open the battery door and wipe it dry.
  • Use the air puffer or drying kit if the supplier provided one.
  • Leave it to dry overnight with the battery door open.
  • Never use heat to dry it.

Poor sound quality

Crackly, distorted or cutting in and out? Try a new battery or full charge first, then brush the microphone openings, then check the filter, dome or tube. If the aid has a programme button, it may have been switched to a different programme. Follow the user guide to return to the normal programme.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Turning the volume up to drown out whistling
  • Ignoring whistling because “it always does that”
  • Rinsing a hearing aid under the tap
  • Using filters or domes from a different make of hearing aid

Persistent wax problems

If wax blocks the filter or earmould day after day, the resident may have wax building up in the ear. Record it and ask the nurse or GP for an ear examination.

Check your understanding

Your answers are not recorded anywhere except, if possible, on this device.

A behind-the-ear hearing aid sounds weak, and you can see small water droplets in the tube. What should you do?

Water droplets block sound. Use the air puffer if supplied, dry gently and leave it open overnight. Never use heat or water on the aid.

Which is the most common cause of whistling (feedback)?

Whistling happens when sound leaks out and is picked up by the microphone, usually because of poor placement, a blockage or a split tube.
