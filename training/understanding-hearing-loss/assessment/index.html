Module 1

Final assessment: Understanding Hearing Loss

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. Where does the 'hearing' of speech actually happen?

    The ears collect sound and turn it into signals, but the brain makes sense of them. This is why conditions such as stroke or dementia can affect understanding.

  2. Which statement best describes age-related hearing loss?

    Age-related hearing loss develops slowly, usually affects both ears, and tends to affect high-pitched sounds such as s, f and th first.

  3. Conductive hearing loss is caused by…

    Conductive hearing loss happens when sound cannot pass through the outer or middle ear. Causes include wax, infection, fluid or a perforated eardrum, and many can be treated.

  4. Why do people with age-related hearing loss often say that others 'mumble'?

    Consonants carry much of the meaning of speech and are often high-pitched and quiet. When they are missed, speech sounds loud enough but unclear.

  5. Which of these could be a sign of hearing loss?

    Turning up the volume and giving unrelated answers are common signs of hearing difficulty. Other signs include asking for repeats and withdrawing from group activities.

  6. Miss Campbell, who lives with dementia, has become tearful and withdrawn at meals over the past fortnight. She seems calmer when spoken to one-to-one in her room.

    What is the best first action?

    Distress in noisy settings can be caused by hearing difficulties. Check hearing aids, reduce background noise, record your observations and report them so her hearing can be checked.

  7. Which statement about hearing loss and dementia is correct?

    Hearing loss can look like confusion and can increase distress. NICE guidance suggests considering hearing assessments for people who have, or may have, dementia.

  8. Mr Lewis tells you at breakfast that he woke up unable to hear in his right ear. Last night he could hear as usual. He has no other symptoms.

    What should happen?

    Sudden hearing loss needs urgent medical assessment. NICE recommends that sudden hearing loss which came on over 3 days or less in the past 30 days is seen by a specialist within 24 hours. Never assume it is wax.

  9. A resident suddenly loses hearing in one ear and you notice one side of their face is drooping. What should you do?

    Hearing loss with facial drooping, weakness, slurred speech or sudden confusion can be a sign of a stroke. Call 999 straight away.

  10. Which information is most useful to record when a resident moves in?

    Recording hearing needs, preferences, equipment, the supplier's contact details and the date of the last hearing test helps everyone support the resident and get help quickly.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
