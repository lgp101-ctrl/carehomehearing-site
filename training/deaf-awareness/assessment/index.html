Module 2

Final assessment: Deaf Awareness

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. What does 'Deaf' with a capital D usually mean?

    'Deaf' with a capital D is used by many people who are part of the Deaf community, with its own language (BSL) and culture.

  2. Which statement about British Sign Language (BSL) is correct?

    BSL is a full language. For many Deaf people it is their first language, so written English may not be fully accessible.

  3. Mrs Kowalski is Deaf and uses BSL. The GP wants to discuss changes to her medication.

    Who should interpret?

    Important conversations, such as about medication, need a qualified, registered interpreter so that information is accurate and private.

  4. When working with a BSL interpreter, you should…

    Speak directly to the resident. The interpreter's job is to interpret. The conversation is between you and the resident.

  5. The Accessible Information Standard requires services in England to…

    The Standard applies to NHS and publicly funded adult social care in England and covers people with a disability, impairment or sensory loss.

  6. Who looks after a resident's cochlear implant?

    Cochlear implants are managed by specialist implant centres. Their contact details should be in the care plan.

  7. Mr Singh nods and says "yes" to everything you say, but then does not do what was agreed.

    What is the most likely explanation, and what should you do?

    People with hearing loss often nod or agree to avoid embarrassment. Check the basics and confirm understanding respectfully.

  8. Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, a resident with hearing loss…

    Capacity is assumed. Giving communication support, such as working hearing aids or an interpreter, is part of the help people must be given to make their own decisions.

  9. Mrs Ellis has decided not to wear her hearing aids because they whistle.

    What should you do?

    Respect her decision, record it, and help solve the problem behind it. Whistling is often caused by fit, wax or tubing problems that can be put right.

  10. A resident with both sight and hearing loss (deafblind) is sitting in the lounge. How should you approach them?

    Deafblind residents need a consistent, gentle way of getting their attention and to know who is with them. Always introduce yourself and say when you are leaving.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
