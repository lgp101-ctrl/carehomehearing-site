Module 8

Final assessment: Referral and Escalation

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. Hearing loss together with face drooping and slurred speech is…

    These are stroke signs. Call 999 immediately.

  2. A resident has had sudden hearing loss in one ear since this morning, with no other symptoms. What is the right pathway?

    Sudden hearing loss is urgent. NICE recommends that sudden loss that came on over 3 days or less in the past 30 days is seen by a specialist within 24 hours.

  3. Which of these is a ROUTINE referral?

    Gradual change with no warning signs is routine. The others need urgent or emergency help.

  4. Who should be contacted about an NHS hearing aid that is still not working after the four-step check?

    Hearing aid faults go to the supplier. NHS audiology services repair NHS hearing aids free of charge.

  5. A resident has ear pain and yellow discharge. What should you do?

    Pain and discharge need same-day medical advice. Keep the hearing aid out until advised.

  6. Hearing that has been rapidly getting worse over several weeks should be…

    NICE recommends urgent referral for hearing loss that is rapidly worsening over 4 to 90 days.

  7. What does SBAR stand for?

    SBAR is a structured way to hand over concerns clearly to a nurse, GP or NHS 111.

  8. A resident may have swallowed a hearing aid battery. What should you do?

    Swallowed button batteries are an emergency because they can cause serious internal injury quickly.

  9. Why must sudden hearing loss never be assumed to be earwax?

    Sudden hearing loss can have causes that need urgent treatment. Assuming it is wax can delay care.

  10. Urgent and emergency ear and hearing problems should go first to…

    Urgent and emergency problems need medical assessment through NHS services. Hearing care providers support routine hearing care.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
