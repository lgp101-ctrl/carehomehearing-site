Personal amplifiers and remote microphones

Two simple tools that make one-to-one conversations much easier, especially in noisy places.

Personal listening amplifiers

A personal amplifier is a small handheld device with a microphone and headphones (or a neckloop for hearing aid users). The speaker talks near the microphone and the resident hears the voice clearly through the headphones.

Good for: residents without hearing aids, residents waiting for hearing aids, one-to-one conversations, GP and care review meetings, family visits.

How to use:

  1. Explain what it is and ask if the resident would like to try it.
  2. Set the volume low before putting the headphones on.
  3. Place the device close to the person speaking, or let them hold the microphone.
  4. Let the resident turn the volume up until it is comfortable.
  5. Speak normally; there is no need to raise your voice.

Hygiene: clean ear cushions and ear pieces between users, following the manufacturer’s instructions and your infection control policy. Some homes give each resident their own headphones.

Remote microphones

A remote microphone is worn by the speaker, for example clipped to a carer’s clothing, or placed on a table. It sends the voice wirelessly to the resident’s hearing aids or to a receiver.

Good for: hearing aid users in noisy rooms, outings, mealtimes and meetings.

  • Many modern hearing aids work with a remote microphone made by the same manufacturer. Ask the hearing aid supplier what is compatible.
  • Charge it with the hearing aids each night.
  • Switch it off or mute it during private conversations or personal care, so the resident does not hear things not meant for them.

Care home scenario

A clearer care review

Mrs Kaur’s care review was always difficult because she could not follow the conversation around the table. Her key worker placed a personal amplifier in the middle of the table and gave Mrs Kaur the headphones. For the first time, she joined in fully and said what mattered to her.

Check your understanding

Your answers are not recorded anywhere except, if possible, on this device.

What must you do before giving a shared personal amplifier to a different resident?

Shared equipment must be cleaned between users. Start at a low volume and let the resident adjust it, to avoid sudden loud sounds.
