Module 7

Final assessment: Assistive Listening Technology

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. Which three things make hearing difficult even with hearing aids?

    Assistive listening technology helps by picking up sound close to its source, cutting out distance, noise and echo.

  2. Which device works well for a resident WITHOUT hearing aids in a one-to-one conversation?

    Personal amplifiers with headphones work for anyone, with or without hearing aids.

  3. What is a remote microphone?

    Remote microphones pick up the speaker's voice close to their mouth and send it straight to the listener.

  4. A hearing aid user gets no benefit from the lounge hearing loop. What should you check?

    Loops only work if the loop is on and the hearing aid's T programme is available and selected.

  5. What is the main benefit of a TV listener?

    TV listeners send sound to headphones or a neckloop so the resident controls their own volume.

  6. How should shared headphones be managed?

    Cleaning between users prevents the spread of infection. Some homes give each resident their own headphones.

  7. What should you do before putting headphones on a resident?

    Starting low avoids sudden loud sounds that could be uncomfortable or distressing.

  8. Which statement about speech-to-text apps is correct?

    Speech-to-text is helpful but imperfect. Check names, numbers and times, and follow confidentiality rules.

  9. What is Relay UK?

    Relay UK uses relay assistants to convert between speech and text for phone calls.

  10. Who can assess a resident for equipment such as vibrating alarms or amplified phones?

    Local council sensory services can assess for, and sometimes provide, equipment. Audiology services and charities can also advise.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
