Module 3

Final assessment: Earwax Management

  • 10 questions, multiple choice
  • Pass mark 80% (8 or more correct)
  • No time limit, and you can try again as often as you like

Get your certificate Back to module

Scroll down to see an explanation for every question.

  1. What is the main job of earwax?

    Earwax protects the ear canal. It usually moves out of the ear by itself and most people never need it removed.

  2. Which of these is safe for a care assistant to do?

    Care staff can clean the outer ear. Nothing should be put inside the ear canal, and care staff should not try to remove wax.

  3. Mrs Begum's hearing aid has been whistling for a week, and its wax filter keeps blocking. She has no pain or other new symptoms.

    What should happen next?

    These signs may suggest wax, but other problems can cause them too. A professional needs to examine the ear. Drops should only be used on professional advice.

  4. Mr Price tells you this morning that he suddenly cannot hear properly in his left ear. He has a history of earwax problems.

    What is the right action?

    Never assume sudden hearing loss is wax, even when someone has had wax before. Sudden hearing loss needs urgent assessment.

  5. Which of these is a warning sign that needs same-day medical advice?

    Ear pain with discharge can be a sign of infection or another problem and needs same-day medical advice. Take the hearing aid out of that ear and ask for advice before using it again.

  6. Who should decide whether a resident uses ear drops?

    Ear drops should only be used on professional advice, following the care plan and medicines policy. They are not suitable for everyone.

  7. Which of these should a professional check before ear drops are used?

    Ear drops may not be suitable if the eardrum is damaged, if there is infection, or after some ear surgery.

  8. According to NICE, which earwax removal method should NOT be used?

    NICE advises against manual syringing. Microsuction, electronic irrigation and other methods may be used by trained professionals.

  9. Why are ear candles and cotton buds not recommended?

    Cotton buds push wax further in and can damage the ear. Ear candles do not remove wax and can cause burns and blockages.

  10. Hearing aids have been taken out because ear drops have been advised by the practice nurse for Miss Ford.

    Which of these should you do?

    Follow the professional's instructions exactly, record each use, and report any problems. Ask how long to wait before putting hearing aids back in.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
