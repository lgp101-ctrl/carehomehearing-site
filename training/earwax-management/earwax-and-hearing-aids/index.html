How earwax affects hearing aids

Earwax is one of the most common reasons hearing aids stop working. Simple daily cleaning, done the right way, keeps them working.

Wax can get into the parts of a hearing aid that sit in or near the ear canal:

  • the earmould sound hole and tubing of a behind-the-ear aid
  • the dome and receiver (speaker) of a receiver-in-canal aid
  • the sound outlet and wax guard of an in-the-ear aid

When wax blocks these, the hearing aid may sound quiet, distorted or completely dead, or may whistle.

Daily care that helps

You will learn how to clean each type of hearing aid in Module 4. In short:

  • wipe hearing aids and earmoulds daily with a dry, soft cloth or the wipes the provider recommends
  • brush away wax from the sound outlet using the small brush supplied
  • check wax filters and domes, and change them when blocked, following the manufacturer’s instructions and the resident’s care plan
  • look at the earmould or dome when you take it out. More wax than usual is worth recording and reporting.

When cleaning hearing aids

  • do not use water on the hearing aid itself (only on a detached earmould, if you have been shown how)
  • do not use alcohol or household cleaners unless the provider has said they are safe
  • do not push anything into the receiver or microphone openings except the tools supplied for that purpose

Wax and hearing appointments

Audiologists need to see the eardrum clearly, and ear impressions for new earmoulds cannot be taken if there is wax in the way. If a resident has an audiology appointment coming up and wax has been a problem, tell the audiology service when you book. They may advise ear drops or an ear examination first.

Care home scenario

A filter that kept blocking

Senior carer Joy noticed Mrs Adeyemi’s left receiver filter was blocked almost every day. She wrote it in the care notes and told the nurse. The practice nurse examined Mrs Adeyemi’s ears and arranged professional wax removal. Afterwards the filters lasted for weeks again, and Mrs Adeyemi said she could hear the birds outside her window.

Which part of a receiver-in-canal hearing aid stops wax getting into the speaker?

The wax filter sits in the sound outlet and catches wax. When it is blocked, sound cannot get out. It should be changed following the manufacturer's instructions.
