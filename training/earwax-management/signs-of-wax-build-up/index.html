Signs of excessive or impacted earwax

Wax build-up has signs you can notice, but many of them can also be caused by other ear problems. Notice, record and report, but never diagnose.

Possible signs of earwax build-up

A resident may have earwax building up if they:

  • say their hearing is muffled or that their ear feels blocked or full
  • seem to hear less well, especially in one ear
  • have hearing aids that whistle (feedback) or sound weak or dead, even with a new battery
  • need their wax filters or domes changed much more often than usual
  • complain of mild discomfort, itching or ringing in the ear (tinnitus)
  • feel slightly dizzy
  • have visible wax at the entrance to the ear canal

These signs are not proof of wax

Many other conditions cause the same signs, including:

  • ear infections
  • a hole in the eardrum
  • fluid behind the eardrum
  • changes in the inner ear or hearing nerve
  • a hearing aid fault

This is why care staff must not diagnose earwax and must never assume that hearing changes are “just wax”.

Never assume sudden hearing loss is wax

Wax build-up usually happens slowly. If hearing changes suddenly, over hours or a few days, the resident needs medical advice the same day, through the GP or NHS 111. Sudden hearing loss can be a sign of a condition that needs urgent treatment, and delays can affect how much hearing comes back.

Record what you notice

Good notes help the nurse, GP or audiologist. For example:

“Right hearing aid whistling since Monday. Wax filter changed twice this week, both blocked with brown wax. Mr Turner says right ear feels ‘full’. No pain, discharge or dizziness. Left ear unchanged.”

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Telling a resident or family “it’s only wax” before anyone has examined the ear
  • Ignoring hearing aid whistling because “it always does that”
  • Not mentioning which ear is affected

Check your understanding

Your answers are not recorded anywhere except, if possible, on this device.

Mr Turner's right hearing aid has started whistling, and you have had to change its wax filter twice this week. He has no pain and no other new symptoms.

What is the most appropriate action?

Whistling and frequently blocked filters may suggest wax build-up, but only a professional can examine the ear and decide. Record and report it.
