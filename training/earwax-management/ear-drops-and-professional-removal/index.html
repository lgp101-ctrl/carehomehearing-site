Ear drops, professional earwax removal and who to contact

Ear drops may be advised to soften wax. If wax needs removing, it must be done by a trained professional. Know what to expect and who to contact.

Ear drops

Ear drops can soften earwax so that it comes out naturally or is easier to remove. Common types include olive oil drops and sodium bicarbonate drops, but the right product and how long to use it should be decided by a professional.

  • Use ear drops only on the advice of a GP, nurse, pharmacist or audiologist.
  • Follow the care plan, the product instructions and your home’s medicines policy. Record each use, for example on the medication record if your policy requires it.
  • NICE guidance notes that softening drops may be used for up to 5 days before professional wax removal.
  • Ear drops are not suitable for everyone. A professional should check first if the resident has, or has had, a perforated eardrum, grommets, ear surgery, an ear infection, discharge or pain.
  • Stop and report if the resident has pain, discharge, dizziness or worse hearing after using drops.
  • Remove hearing aids before drops are used, and ask the professional how long to wait before putting them back in.

Professional earwax removal

If wax needs to be removed, a trained professional will first examine the ear with an otoscope (a small torch with a magnifier) to check the ear canal and eardrum. Methods include:

  • Microsuction: a tiny suction tube removes wax while the clinician looks into the ear through a microscope or magnifier.
  • Electronic irrigation: a controlled flow of warm water washes out the wax, using a machine that controls the pressure.
  • Manual removal: the clinician uses specialist instruments to lift wax out.

NICE advises against manual syringing, an older method where the pressure cannot be controlled.

Who provides earwax removal varies by area:

  • some GP practices and community services
  • some NHS audiology or community ear care services
  • private ear care providers, some of whom visit care homes

Ask your GP practice what is available locally for your residents.

When to contact the GP or audiologist

  • you suspect wax is affecting a resident’s hearing or hearing aids
  • ear drops advised by a professional have not helped
  • wax keeps blocking hearing aids
  • an audiology appointment or ear impressions are coming up
  • straight away for any of the warning signs in the previous lesson

Keep a simple record

Note the date of any ear examination, drops used and wax removal in the resident’s care plan or hearing passport. It helps spot patterns, such as wax that needs attention every few months.

Check your understanding

When should ear drops be used for a resident?

Ear drops are not suitable for everyone, for example people with a perforated eardrum or ear infection. Use them only on professional advice and record their use.

Which method of earwax removal does NICE advise against?

NICE advises that manual syringing should not be used to remove earwax. Microsuction and electronic irrigation by trained professionals are options.
