Quick Help

Who should I contact?

Find the right person or service quickly. Always follow your care home's own escalation policy too.

Your answers:

    What do you need help with?

    • Stroke signs, a head injury, or a swallowed battery
    • Sudden hearing loss, ear pain, discharge or dizziness
    • A hearing aid repair, batteries or new tubing
    • A hearing test or an earwax concern
    • A cochlear implant problem
    • A British Sign Language interpreter
    • Training or support for the whole care home

    Who supplied the hearing aid?

    • The NHS
    • A private provider
    • I am not sure

    Emergency – call 999

    Call 999

    Call 999 for stroke signs (face drooping, weakness, slurred speech, sudden confusion), a head injury with drowsiness, vomiting or fluid from the ear, or a swallowed battery.

    What to do now

    • Follow your emergency procedure.
    • Tell the ambulance crew about the resident's hearing loss.
    • Send hearing aids and the hearing passport if there is time.

    Do not

    • Do not wait to see if it improves.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Urgent – get advice today

    GP or NHS 111, the same day

    Sudden hearing loss, ear pain, discharge, swelling or new dizziness need medical advice today.

    What to do now

    • Contact the GP practice. Out of hours, call NHS 111.
    • Say clearly what has changed and when it started.
    • Record what you did.

    Do not

    • Do not assume sudden hearing loss is wax.
    • Do not book a routine hearing appointment instead.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    The NHS audiology service

    NHS audiology services provide free batteries, repairs, retubing and reviews for the hearing aids they supplied.

    What you can safely check

    • Find the service's contact details in the care plan, hearing passport, NHS hearing aid record book or audiology letters.
    • Ask how they provide batteries and repairs: many offer postal services, drop-in clinics or visits for people who cannot travel.
    • Have the resident's name, date of birth, NHS number and which ear ready.

    Do not

    • Do not send NHS hearing aids to a different provider for repair without the resident's agreement.

    The private provider who supplied it

    Private providers support the hearing aids they sold, often with aftercare included for a period.

    What you can safely check

    • Find the provider's details on the case, charger, paperwork or in the care plan.
    • Ask the family if they arranged the purchase.
    • Ask about home or care home visits if the resident cannot travel.

    Do not

    • Do not assume the NHS will repair privately bought hearing aids.

    Learn more

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Find out who supplied it

    What you can safely check

    • Look on the case, charger and battery packet for a name or logo.
    • Look for an NHS hearing aid record book or audiology letters.
    • Ask the resident and their family.
    • Ask the GP practice, which may have audiology letters on file.
    • Record the details in the care plan once found.

    Do not

    • Do not delay urgent medical advice while you look.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Ask for a hearing assessment or ear examination

    For gradual hearing changes or suspected earwax without warning signs, speak to the nurse, GP practice or audiology service.

    What you can safely check

    • Record what you have noticed.
    • Ask the nurse or GP practice about an ear examination or hearing assessment.
    • In some areas, adults can be referred directly to NHS audiology. Ask locally.
    • The resident or family may also choose a private provider.

    Do not

    • Do not try to remove earwax.
    • Do not use ear drops without professional advice.

    The cochlear implant centre

    Cochlear implants are looked after by specialist implant centres, not hearing aid providers.

    What you can safely check

    • Find the implant centre's details in the care plan or the resident's implant paperwork.
    • Check the processor is charged or has fresh batteries, following the centre's instructions.
    • Contact the implant centre for repairs, spare parts and advice.

    Do not

    • Do not take a cochlear implant processor to a hearing aid provider for repair.

    Get medical help if

    If there is pain, swelling or redness over the implant site, contact the GP the same day.

    Book a registered BSL interpreter

    For important conversations, use a qualified, registered interpreter, not family or untrained staff.

    What you can safely check

    • For NHS appointments, ask the GP practice or hospital to book an interpreter. They are responsible for this.
    • For care reviews and other important conversations, book through your care home's process.
    • Registered interpreters can be found through the NRCPD and similar registers.

    Do not

    • Do not rely on family members for medical or legal conversations.

    Support for your whole care home

    Start with your own team and the free resources here, then look at local NHS and other services.

    What you can safely check

    • Use the free Hearing-Friendly Care Home self-assessment to find your priorities.
    • Appoint a Hearing Loss Champion and use the free toolkit.
    • Ask your local NHS audiology service what support they offer care homes.
    • See all options, including NHS, charity and independent services, on our 'Where to get help' page.
