Quick Help

Their hearing aid has been lost

Search quickly, report it, and contact the right supplier for a replacement.

Your answers:

    Where are you up to?

    • I have not searched yet
    • I have searched and it is still missing
    • The hearing aid or its battery may have been swallowed

    Who supplied the hearing aid?

    Check the care plan, hearing passport, the case, charger or battery packet.

    • The NHS
    • A private provider
    • I am not sure

    You can check this

    Search these places first

    Most lost hearing aids are found within the first hour, in a handful of places.

    What you can safely check

    • Bed, pillowcases, sheets and the floor around and under the bed.
    • The chair the resident sat in, including down the sides and under cushions.
    • Clothing and dressing gown pockets, and the laundry basket.
    • Meal trays, napkins, tissues and bins.
    • The bathroom, and anywhere the resident had personal care.
    • Ask the resident and the staff who last helped them.

    Do not

    • Do not send bedding or clothes to the laundry until checked.
    • Do not empty bins until checked.

    Get medical help if

    If you think the hearing aid or its battery may have been swallowed, call 999 or go to A&E straight away.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Emergency – call 999

    Treat this as an emergency

    Swallowed button batteries can cause serious internal injury within hours.

    What to do now

    • Call 999 or take the resident to A&E straight away, following your emergency procedure.
    • Take the battery packet or the matching hearing aid with you, so staff know the type and size.
    • Tell the nurse in charge.

    Do not

    • Do not wait for symptoms to appear.
    • Do not try to make the resident sick.
    • Do not give food or drink unless a clinician advises it.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Report it and contact the NHS audiology service

    The NHS audiology service that supplied the hearing aid can arrange a replacement.

    What you can safely check

    • Tell the nurse or senior and complete a hearing aid loss incident form.
    • Tell the resident and, if appropriate, their family.
    • Contact the NHS audiology service. Have the resident's details, NHS number and which ear ready.
    • Support communication in other ways until the replacement arrives.
    • Review what happened to prevent it happening again.

    Do not

    • Do not wait several days before reporting it.

    NHS hearing aid

    Some NHS services charge for lost hearing aids, while others replace them free once. Ask about their policy.

    • Download the hearing aid loss incident form

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Report it and contact the private provider

    The provider can arrange a replacement. It may be covered by a warranty or insurance.

    What you can safely check

    • Tell the nurse or senior and complete a hearing aid loss incident form.
    • Tell the resident and, if appropriate, their family, who may have bought the hearing aid.
    • Contact the provider with the make, model and which ear, if known.
    • Support communication in other ways until it is replaced.
    • Review what happened to prevent it happening again.

    Do not

    • Do not wait several days before reporting it.

    Private hearing aid

    Ask whether the hearing aid is covered by a loss warranty or the resident's insurance.

    Learn more

    • Download the hearing aid loss incident form

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Find out who supplied it

    You need to know the supplier before a replacement can be arranged.

    What you can safely check

    • Look in the care plan or hearing passport.
    • Look for a name or logo on the case, charger, battery packet or an NHS hearing aid record book.
    • Look for audiology letters in the resident's records.
    • Ask the resident and their family.
    • If you still cannot find out, the GP practice may have audiology letters on file.
    • Record the supplier's details in the care plan once you know them.

    Do not

    • Do not delay reporting the loss while you find out.
