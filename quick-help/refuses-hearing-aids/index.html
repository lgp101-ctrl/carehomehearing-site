Quick Help

They refuse to wear their hearing aids

Residents can choose not to wear hearing aids, but there is often a reason that can be put right. Respect their choice and look for the cause.

Your answers:

    Do you know why they do not want to wear them?

    • They say they are uncomfortable or sore
    • Too loud, whistling, or 'they don't help'
    • They forget, take them out or lose them (for example, with dementia)
    • They simply do not want to

    Is there pain, redness, swelling, discharge or a sore spot on or in the ear?

    • Yes
    • No, just uncomfortable

    Urgent – get advice today

    Leave the hearing aid out and get the ear checked

    Soreness, redness or discharge needs checking by a doctor or nurse before the hearing aid goes back in.

    What to do now

    • Leave the hearing aid out of that ear and keep it clean and dry.
    • Contact the GP or nurse, today if there is pain, swelling or discharge.
    • Record what you see and which ear.
    • Support communication in other ways meanwhile.

    Do not

    • Do not put the hearing aid back in a sore ear.
    • Do not put anything into the ear.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Uncomfortable but not sore

    Discomfort is often caused by the wrong ear, the wrong angle, or an earmould or dome that needs changing.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the right aid is going in the right ear, and follow the insertion steps.
    • Check the dome is the right size and type.
    • Ask exactly where it hurts or rubs, and record it.

    Do not

    • Do not force the hearing aid in.

    NHS hearing aid

    The NHS audiology service can adjust or remake earmoulds and change domes.

    Private hearing aid

    The private provider can adjust the fit.

    Learn more

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    The settings may need adjusting

    Hearing aids that are too loud, whistle or do not seem to help can usually be improved by the supplier.

    What you can safely check

    • Do the basic checks: battery or charge, blockages, correct ear, fully inserted.
    • Check background noise: many people find hearing aids unpleasant in noisy rooms.
    • Record exactly what the resident says, for example 'everything echoes' or 'cutlery is painfully loud'.
    • Contact the supplier for a review.

    Do not

    • Do not change settings yourself unless the care plan says how.
    • Do not turn the volume up to make it 'help more'.

    NHS hearing aid

    The NHS audiology service can review and adjust the settings.

    Private hearing aid

    The private provider can review and adjust the settings.

    Learn more

    • Hearing aid troubleshooter

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Build a routine that works for them

    People living with dementia may not recognise their hearing aids or remember why they wear them. Routine and the right approach help.

    What you can safely check

    • Look for reasons: discomfort, whistling, noise or a hearing aid that is not working.
    • Put hearing aids in at the same times each day, ideally by staff the resident knows.
    • Try introducing them during an enjoyable activity, such as music or a visit.
    • Ask the supplier about retention cords or simpler devices.
    • Agree an approach with the resident, family and team, and write it in the care plan.

    Do not

    • Do not insist or force them in.
    • Do not give up after one try.

    NHS hearing aid

    The NHS audiology service can advise on devices and settings for people living with dementia.

    Private hearing aid

    The private provider can advise on devices and settings.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident may lack capacity to decide about wearing hearing aids, follow the Mental Capacity Act and your home's best-interests process.

    Learn more

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Respect their choice and keep supporting them

    People can choose not to wear hearing aids. They still need communication support.

    What you can safely check

    • Listen to their reasons and record their decision in the care plan.
    • Use good communication and other aids, such as a personal amplifier, written notes or subtitles.
    • Offer again from time to time, without pressure, as feelings can change.
    • Let them know they can ask the supplier about different styles.

    Do not

    • Do not pressure or shame them.
    • Do not stop offering communication support.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident may lack capacity to make this decision, follow the Mental Capacity Act.
