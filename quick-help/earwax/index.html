Quick Help

Their ears might be blocked with wax

Care staff do not diagnose or remove earwax. This guide helps you decide what to do next safely.

Your answers:

    Do any of these apply?

    • Their hearing changed suddenly
    • They have ear pain, discharge, bleeding, swelling, a temperature or dizziness
    • Hearing loss with face drooping, weakness, slurred speech or sudden confusion
    • None of these

    Do they wear hearing aids?

    • Yes
    • No

    Emergency – call 999

    Call 999 now

    These can be signs of a stroke. Call 999 straight away.

    What to do now

    • Follow your emergency procedure and stay with the resident.
    • Note when the symptoms started.
    • Tell the ambulance crew about the resident's hearing loss.

    Do not

    • Do not assume it is earwax.
    • Do not wait to see if it gets better.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Urgent – get advice today

    Do not assume it is wax: get advice today

    Sudden hearing loss needs medical assessment the same day, even if the resident has had wax problems before.

    What to do now

    • Contact the GP or NHS 111 today, following your escalation policy, and say the hearing loss is sudden.
    • Record which ear is affected and when it started.
    • Communicate using writing, gestures and a quiet space meanwhile.

    Do not

    • Do not start ear drops unless a professional advises it.
    • Do not book a routine wax appointment instead.
    • Do not put anything in the ear.

    Get medical help if

    If stroke signs appear, call 999.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Urgent – get advice today

    Contact the GP the same day

    These symptoms need checking by a doctor or nurse. They may not be caused by wax.

    What to do now

    • Take any hearing aid out of the affected ear and keep it clean and dry.
    • Contact the GP or NHS 111 today.
    • Record what you see and when it started.

    Do not

    • Do not put anything in the ear.
    • Do not use ear drops unless advised.
    • Do not put the hearing aid back in until advised.

    Get medical help if

    Call 999 if the resident has had a head injury and is drowsy or vomiting, or has blood or clear fluid coming from the ear after a fall.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Check the hearing aids and ask for an ear examination

    Wax can block hearing aids and may be building up in the ear. Only a professional can examine the ear and decide what to do.

    What you can safely check

    • Clean the hearing aids and change the wax filter or dome if blocked, following the manufacturer's instructions.
    • Look at the outer ear in good light without putting anything in it.
    • Record what you notice, for example 'filter blocked daily, ear feels full'.
    • Ask the nurse or GP for an ear examination.

    Do not

    • Do not use cotton buds, fingers, tissues or any instrument in the ear.
    • Do not try to remove wax.
    • Do not use ear drops unless a GP, nurse, pharmacist or audiologist has advised them.

    NHS hearing aid

    The GP practice, community ear care service or NHS audiology service can advise on ear examination and wax removal where available.

    Private hearing aid

    Some residents choose private ear care, which may include visits to the care home. This is the resident's choice.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Record it and ask for an ear examination

    If you suspect wax, the next step is a professional ear examination.

    What you can safely check

    • Look at the outer ear in good light without putting anything in it.
    • Clean the outer ear with a damp cloth only.
    • Record what you and the resident notice.
    • Ask the nurse or GP for an ear examination.

    Do not

    • Do not put anything into the ear canal.
    • Do not try to remove wax.
    • Do not use ear drops unless a professional has advised them.

    NHS hearing aid

    The GP practice can advise on ear examination and local wax removal services.

    Private hearing aid

    Some residents choose private ear care. This is the resident's choice.
