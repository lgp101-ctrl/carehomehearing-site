Quick Help

They cannot hear the television

Simple ways to help a resident enjoy TV without turning it up for everyone.

Your answers:

    Where do they watch TV?

    • In their own room
    • In a shared lounge

    Do they wear hearing aids?

    • Yes
    • No

    You can check this

    TV in their room, with hearing aids

    Make sure the hearing aids are working, then try subtitles and equipment that sends TV sound straight to the hearing aids.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the hearing aids are in, working and not blocked.
    • Turn on subtitles. Most TVs have a subtitles button on the remote.
    • Seat them facing the TV, about 2 to 3 metres away, with no window glare.
    • Close the door and turn off other noise.
    • Ask the hearing aid supplier whether a TV streamer can be used. Many modern hearing aids can connect to one.

    Do not

    • Do not keep turning the TV volume up very high, which can disturb others and distort the sound.

    NHS hearing aid

    Ask the NHS audiology service whether their hearing aids can connect to a TV streamer or loop.

    Private hearing aid

    Ask the private provider about TV streaming accessories for the resident's hearing aids.

    Learn more

    • Module 7: Assistive listening technology

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    You can check this

    TV in their room, without hearing aids

    A TV listener with headphones, or a personal amplifier, can make TV much clearer.

    What you can safely check

    • Turn on subtitles.
    • Try a TV listener: a small transmitter connected to the TV that sends sound to headphones, so the resident controls their own volume.
    • Seat them facing the TV and close to it.
    • Record the difficulty and ask about a hearing assessment.

    Do not

    • Do not leave the TV very loud for long periods.

    Learn more

    • Module 7: Assistive listening technology

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    You can check this

    TV in a shared lounge

    Shared lounges are hard for people with hearing loss. A few changes help everyone.

    What you can safely check

    • Turn on subtitles as standard, and ask residents what they prefer.
    • If a hearing loop is fitted, check it is switched on and remind hearing aid users to use the T or loop setting.
    • Offer wireless TV listeners with headphones for residents who want them.
    • Seat residents with hearing loss near the TV and facing it.
    • Keep other noise down: no radio and TV on together, no loud conversations by the TV.
    • Consider a quieter TV lounge for residents who prefer it.

    Do not

    • Do not turn the TV up so loud it is uncomfortable for others.
