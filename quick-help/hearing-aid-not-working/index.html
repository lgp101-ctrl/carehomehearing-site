Quick Help

My resident's hearing aid is not working

Answer one or two questions to find safe checks you can do now, what not to try, and who to contact. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

Your answers:

    What is happening with the hearing aid?

    • No sound at all, or very quiet
    • It whistles or squeals
    • Sound is crackly, distorted or comes and goes
    • It will not charge, or the charger lights look wrong
    • It got wet, or there is water in the tube
    • The tube, dome or wax filter looks blocked
    • It hurts, does not fit or keeps falling out
    • The hearing aid has been lost
    • The resident will not wear it
    • Something else, or I am not sure

    How is the hearing aid powered?

    Battery hearing aids have a small hinged door, usually at the bottom. Rechargeable ones have no door and sit in a charger.

    • It has a battery door
    • It goes in a charger (rechargeable)
    • I am not sure

    Which kind of hearing aid is it?

    • Behind the ear, with a tube and earmould
    • With a thin wire and a dome, or worn inside the ear

    You can check this

    Probably a flat battery

    A flat battery is the most common reason a battery hearing aid goes quiet or stops working.

    What you can safely check

    • Fit a new battery of the correct size. Check the colour on the packet matches the size in the care plan.
    • Peel off the sticky tab. Many manufacturers advise waiting about a minute before putting it in.
    • Line up the + side of the battery with the + marking on the battery door, then close the door gently.
    • If the door will not close, take the battery out and turn it over. Do not force it.
    • If there is still no sound, check the tube, dome or wax filter is not blocked.

    Do not

    • Do not force the battery door closed.
    • Do not use a different battery size.
    • Do not leave used batteries where residents could pick them up. They are dangerous if swallowed.

    NHS hearing aid

    If a new battery does not help, contact the NHS audiology service that supplied it. NHS batteries and repairs are free.

    Private hearing aid

    If a new battery does not help, contact the private provider who supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident's hearing seems to have got worse suddenly, even with a working hearing aid, get same-day medical advice from the GP or NHS 111.

    You can check this

    Probably a charging problem

    Rechargeable hearing aids need a full charge each night. Most charging problems are simple to fix.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the charger is plugged in and switched on at the wall.
    • Check each hearing aid is pushed fully into the charger and the charging lights show as described in the user guide.
    • Wipe the charging contacts gently with a dry, soft cloth.
    • Leave the hearing aids to charge for the time the user guide says, usually several hours.
    • Try the hearing aid in the other charging slot, to see whether the problem is the aid or the charger.

    Do not

    • Do not use a charger from a different make or model, or another resident's charger.
    • Do not put a wet hearing aid in the charger.
    • Do not try to open the hearing aid or charger.

    NHS hearing aid

    If it still will not charge, contact the NHS audiology service that supplied it for a repair or replacement charger.

    Private hearing aid

    If it still will not charge, contact the private provider who supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    If the charger is hot, damaged or smells of burning, unplug it, stop using it and report it.

    You can check this

    Check how it is powered, then check the power

    Look for a small hinged battery door, usually at the bottom of the hearing aid. If there is none, it is probably rechargeable.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the care plan or hearing passport, which should say whether it uses batteries or a charger.
    • Battery door: fit a new battery of the size shown on the packet, + side matching the + marking.
    • No door: check it has been in its charger, the charger is switched on at the wall and the lights show as the user guide describes.
    • Then check the tube, dome or wax filter is not blocked.

    Do not

    • Do not force open any part that does not open easily.
    • Do not use a charger or battery meant for a different hearing aid.

    NHS hearing aid

    Contact the NHS audiology service that supplied it. They can tell you how it works and repair it if needed.

    Private hearing aid

    Contact the private provider who supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident's hearing seems to have changed suddenly, get same-day medical advice from the GP or NHS 111.

    You can check this

    Whistling (feedback)

    Whistling happens when sound leaks out of the ear and is picked up again by the microphone. It is usually caused by fit, a blockage or damaged tubing.

    What you can safely check

    • Take the hearing aid out and put it back in carefully, making sure the earmould or dome is fully in the ear.
    • Check the right hearing aid is in the right ear (red = right, blue = left).
    • Check nothing is pressing on it, such as a hat, scarf, pillow or headrest. Brief whistling during a hug is normal.
    • Check the tube is not split or loose, and the dome is the right one and not damaged.
    • Check the earmould, dome or wax filter is not blocked with wax.

    Do not

    • Do not turn the volume up to cover the whistling.
    • Do not push the earmould or dome in forcefully.
    • Do not put anything into the ear canal.

    NHS hearing aid

    If it still whistles when fitted correctly, contact the NHS audiology service. The earmould, tubing or settings may need changing.

    Private hearing aid

    If it still whistles when fitted correctly, contact the private provider who supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    Whistling together with a blocked feeling or more wax than usual may mean wax build-up: ask the GP or nurse for an ear examination. If there is ear pain, discharge or a sudden change in hearing, get same-day medical advice.

    You can check this

    Crackly, distorted or intermittent sound

    Poor sound is often caused by a low battery, a blockage or moisture.

    What you can safely check

    • Fit a new battery, or check the hearing aid is fully charged. A low battery often causes distorted sound.
    • Brush the microphone openings gently with the brush supplied.
    • Check the wax filter, dome or tube for wax or water droplets.
    • If the hearing aid has a programme button, it may be on a different setting. Follow the user guide to return it to the normal programme.

    Do not

    • Do not use water, alcohol or cleaning sprays on the hearing aid.
    • Do not try to open the case or repair it.

    NHS hearing aid

    If the sound is still poor after these checks, contact the NHS audiology service for a repair.

    Private hearing aid

    If the sound is still poor after these checks, contact the private provider for a repair.

    Get medical help if

    If the hearing aid works but the resident is understanding less than before, ask for a hearing review. If hearing changed suddenly, get same-day medical advice.

    You can check this

    Moisture or water

    Water and sweat can stop hearing aids working. Gentle drying often helps.

    What you can safely check

    • Take the hearing aid out and open the battery door or remove the battery.
    • Wipe the outside dry with a soft cloth.
    • For a tube and earmould, use the air puffer if one was supplied to blow out droplets.
    • If the provider supplied a drying kit or dehumidifier, use it as instructed.
    • Leave it to dry overnight with the battery door open, then try a new battery.

    Do not

    • Do not use a hairdryer, radiator, microwave or oven.
    • Do not put a wet rechargeable hearing aid in its charger unless the user guide says this is safe.

    NHS hearing aid

    If it does not work after drying, contact the NHS audiology service for a repair.

    Private hearing aid

    If it does not work after drying, contact the private provider.

    Get medical help if

    If there is fluid coming from the ear itself, rather than water from outside, take the hearing aid out and contact the GP.

    You can check this

    Blocked tube or earmould

    Wax or water in the earmould or tube stops sound reaching the ear.

    What you can safely check

    • Look at the earmould opening and tube for wax or water droplets.
    • Wipe the earmould and gently clear wax from the opening with the brush or tool supplied.
    • Use the air puffer, if supplied, to blow out water droplets.
    • Check the tube is not squashed, twisted, hard, yellow or split.

    Do not

    • Do not push wax further into the tube.
    • Do not put the hearing aid in water. Only wash an earmould after it has been detached, and only if you have been shown how.
    • Do not cut or tape the tube.

    NHS hearing aid

    For new tubing, contact the NHS audiology service. Retubing is free and some services offer drop-in or postal retubing.

    Private hearing aid

    For new tubing, contact the private provider who supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    If there is a lot of wax on the earmould every day, ask the GP or nurse for an ear examination.

    You can check this

    Blocked wax filter or dome

    A blocked wax filter is one of the most common reasons a receiver-in-canal or in-the-ear hearing aid stops working.

    What you can safely check

    • Look at the dome and wax filter for wax.
    • Change the wax filter using the tool supplied, following the manufacturer's instructions.
    • Replace the dome if it is blocked, dirty or damaged, using the same size and type.
    • Gently brush the sound outlet with the brush supplied.

    Do not

    • Do not poke pins or other objects into the receiver.
    • Do not use filters or domes made for a different hearing aid.

    NHS hearing aid

    If you have no filters or domes, contact the NHS audiology service, which supplies them free.

    Private hearing aid

    If you have no filters or domes, contact the private provider who supplied the hearing aid.

    Get medical help if

    If filters block every day, the resident may have wax build-up. Ask the GP or nurse for an ear examination.

    You can check this

    Fit, comfort or insertion problems

    Hearing aids should be comfortable. Problems are often caused by the wrong ear, the wrong angle or a dome that is the wrong size.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the right hearing aid is going in the right ear.
    • Follow the insertion steps in the user guide or Module 4, making sure the earmould or dome is fully in.
    • Check for any red, sore or swollen areas on and around the ear.
    • Ask the resident where it feels uncomfortable.

    Do not

    • Do not force a hearing aid or earmould into the ear.
    • Do not put a hearing aid back into a sore ear.

    NHS hearing aid

    The NHS audiology service can adjust or remake earmoulds and change domes. Contact them if it still does not fit well.

    Private hearing aid

    The private provider can adjust the fit. Contact them if it still does not fit well.

    Get medical help if

    If there is soreness, redness, swelling, pain or discharge, leave the hearing aid out of that ear and contact the GP or nurse.

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    A lost hearing aid

    Search straight away. Most lost hearing aids are found in bedding, laundry, clothing, trays or bins.

    What you can safely check

    • Search the bed, bedding, pillowcases, chair and the floor around them.
    • Check clothing pockets, the laundry, meal trays, tissues and bins.
    • Ask the resident and the staff who last helped them.
    • Tell the nurse or senior and complete an incident form.

    Do not

    • Do not let bedding or clothes go to the laundry before they are checked.
    • Do not wait several days before reporting it.

    NHS hearing aid

    Contact the NHS audiology service to arrange a replacement. Some services charge for lost hearing aids, so ask about their policy.

    Private hearing aid

    Contact the private provider. The hearing aid may be covered by a warranty or insurance.

    Get medical help if

    If the hearing aid or its battery may have been swallowed, treat it as an emergency: call 999 or go to A&E.

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    The resident will not wear it

    Residents can choose not to wear hearing aids, but there is often a reason that can be fixed.

    What you can safely check

    • Ask the resident why, in a quiet moment, and listen.
    • Check for discomfort, whistling, sounds that are too loud, or a hearing aid that is not working.
    • Record their decision and reasons in the care plan.
    • Offer again later without pressure, and use other communication support meanwhile.

    Do not

    • Do not force hearing aids in.
    • Do not label the resident as 'refuses' and stop offering.

    NHS hearing aid

    The NHS audiology service can adjust settings, fit or tubing if the hearing aid is uncomfortable or too loud.

    Private hearing aid

    The private provider can adjust settings or fit.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident may lack capacity to make this decision, follow the Mental Capacity Act and your home's policy.

    You can check this

    Basic checks for any hearing aid problem

    These checks fix most problems. If they do not, contact the supplier.

    What you can safely check

    • Is it switched on, with a new battery or a full charge?
    • Is the tube, dome or wax filter clear of wax and water?
    • Is it in the right ear and fully inserted?
    • Is it clean and undamaged?
    • Check the user guide, or ask a senior or your Hearing Loss Champion.

    Do not

    • Do not open the case or try to repair it.
    • Do not use water or alcohol on the hearing aid.

    NHS hearing aid

    Contact the NHS audiology service that supplied it.

    Private hearing aid

    Contact the private provider that supplied it.

    Get medical help if

    If the resident's hearing seems to have changed suddenly, or they have ear pain, discharge or dizziness, get same-day medical advice from the GP or NHS 111.
