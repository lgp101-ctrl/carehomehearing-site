Quick Help

They struggle during group activities

Groups are one of the hardest places to hear. Choose the type of activity for practical tips.

Your answers:

    What kind of activity is it?

    • A talk, quiz, discussion or meeting
    • Music, a film or entertainment
    • Mealtimes
    • An exercise class or outing

    You can check this

    Talks, quizzes and discussions

    What you can safely check

    • Check hearing aids are in and working before the session.
    • Seat residents with hearing loss near the leader, facing them, with light on the leader's face.
    • Use the hearing loop or a microphone with a speaker if you have one.
    • Make sure only one person speaks at a time, and repeat questions from the group before answering.
    • Write key words, quiz questions or answers on a board or large cards.
    • Turn off background music.

    Do not

    • Do not talk while facing the board or walking around behind people.
    • Do not let several conversations happen at once.

    Learn more

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    You can check this

    Music, films and entertainment

    What you can safely check

    • Turn on subtitles for films.
    • If a hearing loop is fitted, switch it on and remind hearing aid users to use the T or loop setting.
    • Seat residents with hearing loss near the speakers or performer, but not right next to a loud speaker.
    • Ask residents whether the volume is comfortable. Some people find loud music painful.
    • Introduce each song or film with a clear spoken or written title.

    Do not

    • Do not play music very loudly, which can be uncomfortable for hearing aid users.
    • Do not talk over the music.

    Learn more

    • Module 7: Assistive listening technology

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    You can check this

    Mealtimes

    What you can safely check

    • Seat residents with hearing loss away from the kitchen, serving hatch and doors.
    • Use smaller tables where possible, so everyone can see each other's faces.
    • Turn background music down or off.
    • Show menu choices or plated meals rather than reading them out.
    • Check hearing aids are in before the meal.

    Do not

    • Do not ask about choices from behind the resident.
    • Do not rush answers.

    Learn more

    • Module 6: Communication in daily care

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    You can check this

    Exercise classes and outings

    What you can safely check

    • Demonstrate each movement so people can copy what they see, not just what they hear.
    • Stand where everyone can see your face, and keep music quieter while you give instructions.
    • Agree simple hand signals, such as 'stop' and 'change'.
    • On outings, keep residents with hearing loss close to a staff member and brief them before you set off.
    • Take spare batteries or make sure hearing aids are fully charged.

    Do not

    • Do not give instructions while facing away or over loud music.
