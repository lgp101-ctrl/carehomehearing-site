Quick Help

My resident cannot hear me

Find out whether this needs urgent medical help, a hearing aid check or better communication support.

Your answers:

    What best describes the problem?

    • Their hearing changed suddenly (in the last few hours or days)
    • They have ear pain, discharge, bleeding or dizziness
    • It has been gradual, or I am not sure

    Do they also have any of these: face drooping, weakness in an arm or leg, slurred or muddled speech, or sudden confusion?

    • Yes, one or more of these
    • No

    Do they normally wear hearing aids?

    • Yes
    • No

    Are the hearing aids in, switched on and working?

    • Yes, they seem to be working
    • No, or I am not sure

    Emergency – call 999

    Call 999 now

    Hearing loss with face drooping, weakness, slurred speech or sudden confusion can be a sign of a stroke. Call 999 straight away.

    What to do now

    • Stay with the resident and follow your care home's emergency procedure.
    • Note the time the symptoms started or were first noticed.
    • Tell the ambulance crew about the resident's hearing loss, and send their hearing aids and hearing passport with them if there is time.

    Do not

    • Do not wait to see if it gets better.
    • Do not give food or drink until they have been assessed.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Urgent – get advice today

    Get medical advice the same day

    Sudden hearing loss needs urgent assessment. NICE recommends that hearing loss which came on over 3 days or less in the past 30 days is seen by a specialist within 24 hours.

    What to do now

    • Contact the GP or NHS 111 today, following your escalation policy. Say that the hearing loss is sudden and when it started.
    • Quickly check that any hearing aid is switched on and not blocked, but do not let this delay getting advice.
    • Record what happened, which ear is affected and when it started.
    • Use writing, gestures and a quiet space to communicate in the meantime.

    Do not

    • Do not assume it is earwax.
    • Do not book a routine hearing test instead.
    • Do not wait several days to see if it improves.

    Get medical help if

    If new stroke signs appear, such as face drooping, weakness or slurred speech, call 999.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Urgent – get advice today

    Contact the GP the same day

    Ear pain, discharge, bleeding or dizziness with hearing problems need to be checked by a doctor or nurse.

    What to do now

    • Take out any hearing aid from the affected ear and keep it clean and dry.
    • Contact the GP or NHS 111 today, following your escalation policy.
    • Record what you see, which ear is affected, and when it started.
    • Make sure the resident is safe to move about if they feel dizzy.

    Do not

    • Do not put anything into the ear.
    • Do not use ear drops unless a professional has advised them.
    • Do not put the hearing aid back in until advised.

    Get medical help if

    Call 999 if they have had a head injury and are drowsy or vomiting, have clear fluid or blood from the ear after a fall, or show stroke signs.

    Always follow your care home's policies, the manufacturer's instructions and the resident's care plan.

    Start again

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Improve communication and ask for a hearing review

    If hearing aids are working but the resident still struggles, communication support helps now, and a hearing review may help in the longer term.

    What you can safely check

    • Move somewhere quieter, turn off the TV and close the door.
    • Get their attention, face them at the same level and keep light on your face.
    • Speak clearly without shouting. Rephrase rather than repeat.
    • Write down key words, times and names.
    • Record the difficulty and ask the nurse or GP about a hearing review with the audiology service or provider.

    Do not

    • Do not shout.
    • Do not say 'never mind'.
    • Do not assume the resident is confused.

    NHS hearing aid

    For NHS hearing aids, ask the NHS audiology service for a review. In some areas the GP needs to refer.

    Private hearing aid

    For private hearing aids, contact the provider for a review.

    You can check this

    Check the hearing aids first

    A hearing aid that is out, switched off, flat or blocked is the most common reason a resident suddenly seems unable to hear.

    What you can safely check

    • Check the hearing aids are in the right ears and fully inserted.
    • Fit a new battery or check the charge.
    • Check the tube, dome or wax filter is not blocked.
    • Use the hearing aid troubleshooter for step-by-step checks.

    Do not

    • Do not put anything into the ear canal.
    • Do not open or try to repair the hearing aid.

    NHS hearing aid

    If the checks do not help, contact the NHS audiology service that supplied the hearing aids.

    Private hearing aid

    If the checks do not help, contact the private provider.

    Get medical help if

    If hearing seems to have changed suddenly, get medical advice the same day from the GP or NHS 111.

    Routine – arrange in the coming days

    Support communication and ask for a hearing assessment

    Gradual hearing difficulties in someone without hearing aids are a reason to ask for a hearing assessment.

    What you can safely check

    • Use good communication: quiet space, face to face, clear speech, extra time.
    • Try writing key information or using a personal listening amplifier if your home has one.
    • Record what you notice and ask the nurse, manager or GP about a hearing assessment.
    • Check whether they have hearing aids that are not being used, for example in a drawer.

    Do not

    • Do not assume nothing can be done because of age or dementia.
    • Do not shout.

    NHS hearing aid

    The GP can refer to the NHS audiology service. In some areas adults can be referred directly.

    Private hearing aid

    The resident or their family may choose to contact a private hearing care provider.
