Assessor visit checklist

Care home: ______________________________ Date of visit: ______________ Assessor: ______________________________

I declare that I have had no paid work or sales with this care home in the last 12 months. Signed: ______________________________

1. Staff knowledge

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
All care staff have completed basic hearing care training. (key) Training records show every care staff member has completed basic hearing care training; staff on shift confirm it. ☐ ☐ ☐
New staff receive hearing care training as part of their induction. The induction programme includes hearing care, and recent starters' records show they completed it. ☐ ☐ ☐
Staff know how to put in, take out and clean each type of hearing aid used in the home. (key) Two or three staff show how they put in, take out and clean the types of hearing aid residents use. ☐ ☐ ☐
Staff recognise the signs of hearing loss and know when and how to escalate concerns. Staff describe the signs of hearing loss and the home's routine, urgent and emergency routes for concerns. ☐ ☐ ☐
Hearing care training is refreshed at least once a year and completions are recorded. Records show hearing care training refreshed within the last 12 months and completions logged. ☐ ☐ ☐

2. Hearing aid maintenance

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
Every resident's hearing aids are checked daily to make sure they are in, working, clean and comfortable. (key) Daily check records for hearing aid users are complete; aids seen on residents are in, working and clean. ☐ ☐ ☐
The right batteries are always in stock, and chargers are working and switched on. The right batteries are in stock and in date; chargers are working and in use. ☐ ☐ ☐
Hearing aids are kept in labelled cases in an agreed place, written in the care plan. Hearing aids are kept in labelled cases in the place each care plan states. ☐ ☐ ☐
Tubing, domes and wax filters are checked and replaced when needed. Tubing, domes and wax filters seen on aids are in good condition; spares are available or ordered. ☐ ☐ ☐
Lost hearing aids are recorded as incidents and reviewed to prevent repeats. Lost hearing aids are logged as incidents, with actions taken to prevent repeats. ☐ ☐ ☐

3. Referral procedures

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
Staff know that sudden hearing loss, ear pain, discharge or new dizziness need medical advice the same day. (key) Staff say that sudden hearing loss, ear pain, discharge or new dizziness need medical advice the same day; the escalation guide is on display. ☐ ☐ ☐
There is a clear, agreed route for arranging hearing assessments and ear examinations. A written route for arranging hearing assessments and ear examinations is known to senior staff and has been used. ☐ ☐ ☐
Each hearing aid user's supplier (NHS service or private provider) and contact details are recorded. (key) Each hearing aid user's NHS service or private provider and contact details are recorded in their care plan. ☐ ☐ ☐
Hearing needs and hearing aids are included in hospital transfer information. Hospital transfer information includes hearing needs and hearing aids; an example transfer form is seen. ☐ ☐ ☐
Hearing is asked about when residents move in and reviewed at care reviews. Admission assessments ask about hearing, and care reviews show hearing reviewed. ☐ ☐ ☐

4. Communication practices

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
Each resident's communication needs and preferences are recorded and followed. (key) Communication needs and preferences are recorded in care plans and match what staff describe and do. ☐ ☐ ☐
Staff get residents' attention, face them and speak clearly without shouting. Staff are seen getting residents' attention, facing them and speaking clearly without shouting. ☐ ☐ ☐
Notepads, whiteboards, pictures or captions are available and used where helpful. Notepads, whiteboards, pictures or captions are available in shared areas and used where helpful. ☐ ☐ ☐
Registered BSL interpreters are booked for important conversations where needed. The home knows how to book a registered BSL interpreter and has done so where needed. ☐ ☐ ☐
Residents living with dementia and hearing loss have individual communication plans. Residents living with dementia and hearing loss have individual communication plans that staff follow. ☐ ☐ ☐

5. Acoustic environment

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
There is at least one quiet space for conversations and visits. (key) At least one quiet space is available and used for conversations and visits. ☐ ☐ ☐
TVs, radios and music are turned down or off when no one is actively enjoying them. During the visit, TVs, radios and music are off or low when no one is watching or listening. ☐ ☐ ☐
Lounges and dining rooms have soft furnishings that reduce echo. Lounges and dining rooms have soft furnishings that reduce echo. ☐ ☐ ☐
Lighting is good, including in the evening, so residents can see faces. Lighting in shared areas lets residents see faces clearly, including in the evening. ☐ ☐ ☐
Fire and emergency alerts are suitable for residents who cannot hear alarms. (key) Residents who cannot hear alarms have visual or vibrating alerts, or a written personal evacuation plan that covers this. ☐ ☐ ☐

6. Assistive listening equipment

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
Personal listening amplifiers are available for residents without hearing aids. Personal listening amplifiers are available, working and offered to residents without hearing aids. ☐ ☐ ☐
Subtitles and TV listeners are offered to residents who struggle with the TV. Subtitles or TV listeners are offered and in use for residents who struggle with the TV. ☐ ☐ ☐
Hearing loops are fitted where useful and checked regularly. Hearing loops, where fitted, work when tested, and checks are recorded. ☐ ☐ ☐
Shared equipment is cleaned between users and kept charged. Shared listening equipment is clean and charged, with a cleaning routine in place. ☐ ☐ ☐

7. Family involvement

Criterion What to look for Met Not met N/A Notes
Families are asked about their relative's hearing and hearing aids when they move in. Admission records show families are asked about their relative's hearing and hearing aids. ☐ ☐ ☐
Families know who to talk to about hearing care. Information given to families names who to talk to about hearing care. ☐ ☐ ☐
Families are given practical tips for communicating during visits. Families are given practical tips for communicating during visits, for example a leaflet or a poster. ☐ ☐ ☐
Families are kept informed about hearing aid problems, losses and appointments. Records show families are told about hearing aid problems, losses and appointments. ☐ ☐ ☐

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
