Hearing Loss Champion programme

A Hearing Loss Champion is a member of staff who leads hearing care day to day. This advanced pathway prepares you for the role.

The Champion's responsibilities

  1. Encourage daily hearing aid checks. Make sure every hearing aid user has their aids checked, cleaned and worn every day, and that the daily checklist is used.
  2. Support colleagues. Show colleagues how to care for hearing aids and communicate well, and be the go-to person for questions.
  3. Identify residents who need professional support. Spot residents who may need a hearing assessment, an ear examination or a hearing aid review, and make sure it happens.
  4. Maintain hearing care equipment. Keep batteries, cases, cleaning supplies, amplifiers, TV listeners and loops ready and working.
  5. Coordinate appropriate referrals. Work with the nurse, GP, NHS audiology service and suppliers so residents get the right help at the right time.
  6. Monitor hearing accessibility. Keep an eye on noise, lighting, quiet spaces and alerts, using the environment audit.
  7. Help embed hearing care into daily routines. Make hearing part of care plans, induction, handovers, care reviews and hospital transfers.

Not part of the role

Champions do not examine ears, remove earwax, diagnose or repair hearing aids. Urgent problems always go to the nurse in charge, GP, NHS 111 or 999.

The pathway

Step 1: Complete all eight modules

  1. Understanding Hearing Loss (16 minutes)
  2. Deaf Awareness (18 minutes)
  3. Earwax Management (20 minutes)
  4. Hearing Aid Management (20 minutes)
  5. Hearing Aid Troubleshooting (19 minutes)
  6. Communication Skills in Care Homes (21 minutes)
  7. Assistive Listening Technology (19 minutes)
  8. Referral and Escalation (19 minutes)

Step 2: Get to know your home

  • Complete the self-assessment with your manager.
  • Walk round with the environment audit.
  • List every resident with hearing needs, their equipment and supplier.
  • Find your local contacts: GP practice, NHS audiology, suppliers, interpreters and council sensory services.

Step 3: Lead the routines

  • Introduce the daily hearing aid checklist on every unit.
  • Complete the weekly hearing care checklist.
  • Start a hearing passport for each resident with hearing needs.
  • Run a 10-minute team huddle on one hearing topic each month.

Step 4: Review and improve

  • Review lost hearing aids and referrals monthly.
  • Repeat the self-assessment and audit each year and share progress.
