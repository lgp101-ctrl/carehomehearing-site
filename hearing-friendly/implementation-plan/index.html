Implementation plan for managers

A suggested plan for introducing a Hearing Loss Champion and making hearing care part of everyday life. Adapt it to your home's size and priorities. You can tick off steps on the manager dashboard.

First two weeks

1. Get started

  • Appoint a Hearing Loss Champion, and a deputy if possible
  • Agree protected time for the Champion role each week
  • Complete the Hearing-Friendly Care Home self-assessment together
  • List every resident with hearing needs, their equipment and who supplied it

First month

2. Build the foundations

  • All care staff start the core training modules
  • Introduce the daily hearing aid checklist on every unit
  • Start a hearing passport for each resident with hearing needs
  • Stock the right batteries, labelled cases and cleaning supplies

Months two and three

3. Embed it in everyday care

  • Complete the environment audit and act on the quick wins
  • Agree referral routes with your GP practice and NHS audiology service
  • Add hearing to induction, care reviews and hospital transfer paperwork
  • Introduce the Champion to families and share the family resources

Ongoing

4. Keep it going

  • Champion completes the weekly hearing care checklist
  • Review lost hearing aids and referrals every month
  • Repeat the self-assessment and refresher training every year
  • Celebrate progress with staff, residents and families

Tips for success

  • Protect the Champion's time. Even one hour a week makes a difference.
  • Start small. Daily hearing aid checks on every unit are the single biggest win.
  • Make it visible. Display the communication tips poster and talk about hearing in handovers.
  • Involve residents and families. Ask what matters to them.
  • Measure progress. Repeat the self-assessment and compare results.

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
