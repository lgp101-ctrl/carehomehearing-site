Care home hearing environment audit

Walk around your home at a busy time of day and answer these questions on your phone. You will get an action plan for making the building easier to hear in. Prefer paper? Print the audit.

This is a self-assessment. It is not an independent assessment, inspection or certification, and completing it does not mean your care home is accredited or approved by anyone.

Unanswered questions are treated as "not yet in place". Your answers are saved on this device only.

1. Lounges and activity rooms

Walk through each lounge at a busy time of day.

Background noise is low enough to hold a conversation without raising your voice.
The TV and music are not on at the same time.
There are soft furnishings, such as curtains, rugs and cushions, to reduce echo.
Seating is arranged in small groups so residents can see each other's faces.
Lighting is good and nobody has to face a bright window to see others.
If a hearing loop is fitted, it works and the loop sign is displayed.

2. Dining areas

Tables for residents with hearing loss are away from the kitchen, serving hatch and doors.
Background music is off or low during meals.
Tables are small enough for residents to see and hear each other.
Menus are shown or written down rather than only read out.

3. Bedrooms

Each hearing aid user has a labelled case and charger or battery supply in an agreed place.
Chargers are plugged into sockets that are not switched off at night.
Bedside lighting is good enough for lip-reading and handling hearing aids.
Residents who cannot hear doorbells, phones or alarms have suitable alerts.

4. Quiet spaces and visiting

There is a quiet room or corner that residents and visitors can use.
The quiet space has good lighting and chairs that can face each other.
Staff suggest the quiet space to families when they visit a resident with hearing loss.

5. Reception and offices

Reception has a counter hearing loop or a personal amplifier available.
Written information is available for visitors who cannot hear well.
Information for residents and families is available in accessible formats.

6. Safety and emergencies

Every resident's personal emergency evacuation plan records whether they can hear alarms.
There is a plan to alert residents who cannot hear the fire alarm, especially at night.
Hearing aid batteries are stored and disposed of safely, out of reach of residents at risk.
