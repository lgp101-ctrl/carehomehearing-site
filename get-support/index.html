Where to get help

Who to contact for hearing and ear problems, starting with emergencies. Always follow your care home's escalation policy and the resident's care plan.

Emergency: call 999

  • Hearing loss with stroke signs: face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred speech or sudden confusion
  • A head injury with drowsiness, vomiting, or blood or clear fluid from the ear
  • A swallowed battery, including hearing aid batteries

Urgent: GP or NHS 111, the same day

  • Sudden hearing loss or sudden worsening, in one or both ears. Never assume it is wax.
  • Ear pain, discharge, bleeding, swelling or redness
  • New dizziness or a spinning feeling with hearing changes
  • Ear symptoms that are not improving in someone with diabetes or a weakened immune system

Routine help: hearing aids, tests and earwax

Hearing aid repairs, batteries and tubing

Contact whoever supplied the hearing aid. NHS audiology services provide free batteries, repairs and retubing for NHS hearing aids. Private providers support the hearing aids they sold.

Not sure who supplied it?

Hearing tests

Ask the GP for a referral to the NHS audiology service. In some areas adults can be referred directly. Some NHS services visit people who cannot travel. Residents can also choose a private hearing care provider.

How to arrange a hearing assessment

Possible earwax

Ask the nurse or GP practice for an ear examination. Earwax removal is offered by some GP practices, community ear care services and NHS audiology services, and by private ear care providers. Availability varies by area.

Quick Help: earwax

Cochlear implants

Contact the resident's cochlear implant centre. Their details should be in the care plan.

British Sign Language interpreters

For NHS appointments, the GP practice or hospital should book a registered interpreter. Registered interpreters can be found through the NRCPD.

Equipment, information and support

  • Your local council's sensory services team may assess for equipment such as alerting devices and amplified phones.
  • RNID offers free information about hearing loss, tinnitus and equipment.
  • NHS: hearing loss

Professional support from Hear4All, the founder of this academy

Care Home Hearing was created and is funded by Hear4All, a hearing care provider. If your care home would like professional support, such as staff training, hearing screening days or hearing aid maintenance, you can find out about Hear4All's services.

You do not need to use Hear4All to use anything on this website, and NHS services remain available to all residents. For urgent or emergency problems, always use the routes above.

About Hear4All's professional services

This website gives general information and training. It does not replace advice from a GP, nurse, audiologist or other health professional. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or shows signs of a stroke, call 999. For urgent advice, contact their GP or NHS 111.
