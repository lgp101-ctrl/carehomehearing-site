Why hearing care matters in care homes

Hearing loss is one of the most common long-term conditions in later life, and most people living in care homes are affected to some degree. It is also easy to miss.

When hearing is not supported, residents can become withdrawn, lonely or anxious. They may seem confused, miss important information about their care, or stop joining in. Hearing loss can be mistaken for dementia, and it can make the effects of dementia worse.

The good news is that small daily actions make a real difference: a working hearing aid, a quiet spot to talk, and a carer who faces the resident when they speak.

  1. Check hearing aids every day. A flat battery or blocked tube can silence a hearing aid.
  2. Face the person and speak clearly. Most people rely on lip-reading more than they realise.
  3. Act quickly on sudden hearing loss. It needs medical advice the same day. Never assume it is wax.
